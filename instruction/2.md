# Salt Growth Model — M2 Critical Review and Follow-up

Repository: https://github.com/ndmthio18/salt_grow

Review the current `prat_dynamic_growth` implementation before making changes.

## Main finding

The baseline predicts substantial densification (minimum porosity ~0.243) but only ~3% deviation from Prat's analytical growth velocity. The B1/B2 difference is below 0.1%.

We need to determine whether this is an intrinsic consequence of the model assumptions or a limitation of its interface closure.

## Task 1 — Explain the existing model

Audit `dynamic.py`, focusing on:

- Constant `epsilon_birth = 0.43`.
- Independent interfacial kinetic growth law.
- Internal precipitation and porosity evolution.
- Prescribed evaporation flux and reconstructed liquid supply.
- Top boundary use of `epsilon_birth` versus the adjacent evolving porosity.

Explain quantitatively why strong densification behind the growth front produces only weak changes in velocity.

Do not alter the code yet.

## Task 2 — Isolate the source of the 3% deviation

Design controlled comparisons separating:

- Quasi-steady versus transient transport.
- Neglected versus retained advection.
- Linearized versus nonlinear reaction terms.
- Fixed structural coefficients versus evolving porosity.
- Evolving specific surface area and effective diffusivity.

Do not attribute all differences between Model A and Model B to porosity evolution without an appropriate controlled comparison.

Maintain consistent mass conservation and boundary conditions.

## Task 3 — Develop an alternative front-growth closure

Propose a physically defensible alternative to the constant-birth-porosity self-replication assumption.

Distinguish:

1. Solid mass forming new porous layers.
2. Solid mass filling existing pores.
3. Redistribution of dissolved salt through the reaction layer.

Ensure exact salt and solid-volume conservation.

Do not simply set `epsilon_birth = epsilon_top_cell` or tune parameters to increase deviation from Prat.

Explain what additional crystal-scale assumptions or parameters are required.

## Task 4 — Supply limitation

Develop a minimal hydraulic feedback model coupling evolving porosity to permeability and liquid-supply resistance.

Use the resulting supply constraint to assess whether the prescribed evaporation flux is physically sustainable.

Do not interpret a numerical porosity stop event as experimentally validated pore blocking.

## Task 5 — Numerical verification

Improve the sparse implicit solver to permit higher-resolution convergence tests.

Check:

- Local liquid and salt flux residuals.
- Moving-interface balance.
- Newly formed layer solid volume.
- Grid and time convergence.
- Boundary-layer resolution.
- Sensitivity to the initial concentration profile.

## Deliverable

Produce `M2_CRITICAL_REVIEW.md` first, with file/line references, a physics-based explanation of the current results, and a proposed model comparison matrix.

Implement additional models only after defining consistent governing equations and acceptance tests.

The objective is not to force a larger deviation from Prat, but to determine whether internal precipitation can fundamentally change the self-replication growth regime.
