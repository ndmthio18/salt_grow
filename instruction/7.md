# Phase 2A.1 — Stochastic CA Growth and Morphology Verification

Repository: `ndmthio18/salt_grow`

Branch: `codex/phase2a-minimal-ca`

Reviewed commit: `96effabf9bca5c8825d2a158dc7c59feb0dd56c1`

## Objective

The Phase 2A prototype successfully implements a mass-conservative 3D salt growth geometry.

However, its current deterministic coordination-ranking algorithm is not equivalent to the stochastic attachment rules used in the reference Redkov CA model.

Before introducing additional transport physics, we need to determine whether the growth algorithm can produce meaningful, reproducible, and potentially porous three-dimensional salt structures.

This phase focuses on spatial growth rules, timestep consistency, morphology characterization, and numerical verification.

Do not proceed to PNM coupling, CUDA acceleration, vapor diffusion, or permeability calculations.

## Task 1 — Implement stochastic coordination-dependent growth

Preserve the existing deterministic coordination-ranking algorithm as a legacy control.

Introduce a new selectable weighted stochastic growth mode.

For each eligible site, compute the number of adjacent NaCl voxels and assign a configurable attachment weight:

- One crystalline neighbor: w1.
- Two crystalline neighbors: w2.
- Three crystalline neighbors: w3.
- Four or more neighbors: w4.

Sites without a salt neighbor remain ineligible.

Use these weights to perform stochastic candidate selection, with a reproducible NumPy random-number generator.

The weights must influence the spatial distribution of new crystalline voxels but must not change the prescribed cumulative precipitation mass budget.

Do not interpret the weights as physically calibrated NaCl attachment probabilities.

Clearly distinguish weighted sampling under a fixed mass budget from the original Redkov Monte Carlo attachment process.

## Task 2 — Investigate sequential versus synchronous growth

The current implementation selects all growth sites from a frozen candidate list within each timestep.

This may cause artificial dependence on the physical timestep.

Implement a controlled comparison between:

A. Existing synchronous batch selection.

B. Sequential event-based selection, in which candidate eligibility and coordination are updated after each realized voxel addition.

For the sequential algorithm, preserve the random-number-generator state across physical timesteps instead of reseeding at each step.

Compare 30 s, 60 s, and 120 s timesteps under identical total simulation duration, evaporation flux, initial seeds, total salt supply, and spatial rules.

Track differences in total solid mass, maximum height, occupied footprint, crystal connectivity, and final voxel morphology.

Where the sequential model has no other explicit time-dependent physics, investigate whether its final geometry depends only on the cumulative number of deposition events and random-number sequence.

Do not require exact spatial equality for different independent stochastic realizations; use paired-seed comparisons and statistical diagnostics.

## Task 3 — Systematic growth-rule sensitivity

Perform a small controlled comparison of at least:

- Uniform weights across all eligible coordination classes.
- Strongly preferential growth at high-coordination sites.
- Less preferential growth at high-coordination sites.
- The original deterministic ranking as a reference.

Choose and document exact values for each weight set.

Keep the total committed salt volume identical when comparing morphologies, unless a spatial growth constraint prevents complete realization.

If full realization is impossible, report retained mass and stop or flag the comparison; do not compare unmatched inventories as if they were equivalent.

Run several reproducible random seeds for each stochastic configuration.

Report the variation between realizations instead of presenting one morphology as a representative physical prediction.

## Task 4 — Crystal nucleation and spatial connectivity

Compare different initial seed configurations on the same substrate.

At minimum, examine single-seed and multiple-seed cases.

Measure:

- Number of disconnected crystalline clusters.
- Cluster sizes.
- Crystal coalescence events.
- Maximum growth height.
- Lateral footprint.
- Connectedness to the initially wetted seeds.

Do not allow initially dry and disconnected salt clusters to grow under the enforced-wetting condition.

Retain a separate nonphysical wetting-disabled control.

Clearly distinguish connectivity of the solid salt network from actual connectivity of a liquid film.

## Task 5 — Characterize emerging void morphology

The main scientific question is whether a realistic porous-salt-like geometry can emerge from the growth rule.

Save actual 3D solid masks at selected times, not only lists of coordinates.

Provide:

- 3D voxel morphology visualizations.
- Horizontal and vertical cross sections.
- Salt volume fraction within explicitly defined analysis windows.
- Salt cluster size distributions.
- Void-space connected components.
- Void connectivity to the external air boundary.
- Basic internal or enclosed void statistics.

Do not treat all air outside the growing crystal structure as internal salt porosity.

If defining an intrinsic porous-salt envelope requires an additional assumption, test more than one reasonable definition and clearly describe the sensitivity.

Avoid reporting a single intrinsic porosity value when the geometry does not support a robust definition.

Do not yet calculate permeability or effective diffusion coefficients.

## Task 6 — Numerical correctness and failure handling

Preserve the exact distinction between initial seed mass and newly precipitated salt mass.

Verify the geometric inventory directly against the transactional mass ledger after each growth step.

Ensure that all deferred or unrealized mass remains explicitly accounted for.

Test that no crystalline voxel can overwrite the original substrate.

Review the exception handling in the geometry update.

A failed candidate-selection, geometry-update, or commit operation must not leave a pending transaction or an inconsistent geometry/ledger state.

Avoid silent mass loss and artificial crystal creation.

Add automated tests for timestep comparison, stochastic reproducibility, controlled differences between growth rules, insufficient spatial capacity, wetting connectivity, and transaction rollback.

## Task 7 — Demonstration and scientific assessment

Use a small CPU-accessible domain with sufficient vertical space.

Choose a simulation horizon that does not exceed geometric capacity.

For each controlled case, provide:

- Input configuration.
- Numerical runtime.
- Conservation history.
- Final salt volume.
- Maximum growth height.
- Footprint.
- Solid and void connectivity.
- At least one interpretable 3D rendering and two cross sections.

Compare morphologies at identical precipitated solid volumes.

Critically evaluate whether the growth rules produce dense crystalline clusters, open branches, connected crystal aggregates, or potentially porous structures.

Do not claim that any resulting morphology is representative of experimental NaCl without independent evidence.
