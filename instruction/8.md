# Phase 2A.2 — Morphology Feasibility and Numerical Robustness Audit

Repository: ndmthio18/salt_grow

Base commit: 098f5bb8cec41ab28502fa3d6b1126057a14901a

## Objective

Evaluate whether the current mass-conservative sequential CA can produce geometrically meaningful porous-salt structures suitable for future liquid transport simulations.

Do not implement a PNM solver, vapor diffusion solver, or permeability calculation at this stage.

## Task 1 — Fix sequential transaction rollback

Review `SaltGrowthCA._sequential_selection()` and `grow()`.

There is a potential rollback failure if an exception occurs after some voxels have been added inside `_sequential_selection()` but before the function returns its selected-coordinate list.

Make geometry updates transactional using a tentative geometry or an exception-safe update log.

Ensure failures during candidate generation, stochastic selection, individual voxel insertion, and commit leave geometry, ledger, pending transaction state, and RNG state unchanged.

Add failure-injection tests after the first and several subsequent sequential insertion events.

## Task 2 — Distinguish external air from porous pathways

Review `morphology.py`.

Do not treat all void voxels in an axis-aligned bounding box as intrinsic porous-salt pores.

Implement diagnostics for:

- Solid cluster connectivity.
- Void-space connected components.
- Bottom-to-top void percolation.
- Bottom-to-lateral boundary connectivity.
- External-air-accessible voids.
- Enclosed voids.
- Accessible pore volume under explicitly defined analysis masks.

Distinguish computational-box percolation from transport pathways inside a physically meaningful salt-structure envelope.

Evaluate multiple envelope definitions and explicitly flag cases in which intrinsic porosity is not uniquely identifiable.

No permeability calculations yet.

## Task 3 — Grid and geometry sensitivity

Use the stochastic sequential algorithm as the primary numerical mode.

Compare voxel sizes of 5, 10, and 20 µm while keeping the physical domain dimensions, evaporation area, physical duration, and total supplied salt mass consistent.

Ensure that initial seed size and positions represent comparable physical configurations rather than arbitrarily changing physical seed dimensions with voxel size.

Use a small ensemble of pinned random seeds.

Evaluate changes in:

- Crystal height and footprint in physical units.
- Solid connectivity.
- Void connectivity.
- Window-based solid-volume fractions.
- Surface-to-volume ratio.
- Characteristic solid feature size.
- Computational runtime.

Clearly state whether the morphology has converged or remains dependent on the voxel scale.

Do not interpret identical total mass as evidence of morphology convergence.

## Task 4 — Physical growth-rule assessment

Investigate whether the current six-face coordination weights can be interpreted as coarse-grained morphology parameters.

Do not equate 10-µm voxel coordination with atomistic kink attachment kinetics.

Identify the minimal additional physical ingredients potentially required for porous NaCl growth, focusing on:

- Crystal grain identity and orientation.
- Multiple interacting crystals.
- Surface-film-supported growth.
- Local supply accessibility.
- Intercrystalline void preservation.

Prioritize physical justification rather than adding many uncalibrated empirical parameters.

## Task 5 — Morphology comparison

Reuse the Phase 2A.1 configurations and saved masks where possible.

Compare compact high-coordination structures with more open stochastic aggregates at matched salt inventory.

Quantify whether any case exhibits a continuous pore pathway suitable for future liquid supply.

Do not infer pore permeability from porosity or connectivity alone.

Provide 3D renderings and cross sections that clearly distinguish salt, substrate, exterior air, and candidate internal pore space.
