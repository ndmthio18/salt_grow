# Phase 2A.2R — Correct Pore Diagnostics and Design a Grain-Aware CA

Repository: `ndmthio18/salt_grow`

Base commit: `3f6fc075e88fed9a175960554b8325c13ccb2045`

## Objective

The Phase 2A.2 numerical study has demonstrated mass conservation but has not established mesh-independent porous NaCl morphology.

Before implementing new growth physics, correct the pore-connectivity diagnostics and develop a minimal, physically motivated grain-growth design.

Do not implement permeability, vapor diffusion, full liquid transport, or PNM coupling.

## Task 1 — Correct the column-envelope percolation test

Review `column_envelope()` and `_boundary_masks()` in `morphology.py`.

The current column envelope terminates each occupied column at its highest salt voxel. Consequently, the global upper boundary of that envelope may contain no empty voxels, making bottom-to-top void percolation false by construction.

Demonstrate this property analytically and with an automated test.

Replace this inappropriate boundary definition with explicit physical inlet/outlet surfaces.

Keep distinct:

- Connection to exterior air.
- Intercrystalline gaps.
- Geometric bottom-to-top paths.
- A physically justified liquid-supply pathway.

Do not infer liquid connectivity from exterior-air connectivity.

## Task 2 — Establish independent geometry benchmarks

Create small synthetic voxel geometries with known connectivity:

1. A solid pillar surrounded by open air.
2. A porous slab containing a known through-channel.
3. Two solid grains separated by a connected intergranular gap.
4. A closed internal cavity.
5. An externally connected blind pore.

For each geometry, calculate connectivity under explicitly defined physical boundaries.

The diagnostic must distinguish an exterior bypass around the solid from a channel through the intended porous material.

Provide expected analytical or geometric reference answers and automated tests.

## Task 3 — Correct intrinsic porosity reporting

Do not define porosity identifiability merely by exact equality of different envelope masks.

Separate:

- Fixed-window solid volume fraction.
- Envelope-dependent void fraction.
- Accessible intergranular void volume.
- Enclosed void volume.
- Unresolved or ambiguous exterior space.

Report envelope sensitivity quantitatively.

For shell-based definitions, use a fixed physical shell thickness rather than one voxel when comparing grid resolutions.

Do not assign a unique intrinsic porosity if the material boundary remains ambiguous.

## Task 4 — Design a minimal grain-aware growth model

Develop a technical design, without full implementation, for extending the current sequential CA with:

- Persistent crystal grain IDs.
- Crystal orientation.
- Facet-dependent growth preferences.
- Grain impingement and contact.
- Explicit distinction between solid-salt connectivity and liquid-film accessibility.
- Conservation of the existing evaporation-derived salt budget.

The model should contain as few free parameters as reasonably possible.

Identify which parameters have physical dimensions and which require experimental calibration.

Explain how the same physical crystal size and orientation would be represented across 5, 10, and 20 µm voxel grids.

Explicitly discuss which physical mechanisms could preserve intercrystalline pores rather than filling all available gaps.

Do not use an arbitrarily prescribed target porosity as a growth rule.

## Task 5 — Assess the required physical evidence

Determine the minimum experimental observations needed to constrain the grain-aware CA.

Focus on:

- Crystal or grain size distribution.
- Facet orientation and shape.
- Grain-contact geometry.
- Intergranular gap distribution.
- Structural changes with growth.
- Surface-film evidence where available.

Separate parameters that could be calibrated using 2D SEM or optical images from quantities that genuinely require 3D morphology measurements.

Do not claim that existing experimental data are available unless verified.
